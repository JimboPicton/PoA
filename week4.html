Ask the room

When the ball changes direction, which part should change direction last?

Williams and Blair, in practice

The body leads. The tail catches up.

Follow through is the continuation of flexible parts after the main body changes speed or direction. Track the alternating tail-up, tail-down and passing poses to see how the action travels from the attachment to the tip.

Try this

Set Tail delay to zero, then raise it to three frames. Watch the launch, apex and landing. Which version feels more flexible?

Study alongside Richard Williams’ discussion of flexibility, successive breaking of joints and overlapping action. Preston Blair also advises that parts should arrive at different times and that loose parts require clear follow-through (printed pp. 128, 134 and 221).
